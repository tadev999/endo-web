# The Mountain Looks Scariest When You Stand Looking Up from Below — Look Down and See How Far You Have Traveled

*Article 76 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

On certain days you feel small before life.

You stand there, gazing up at the mountain ahead. The peak stands vague concealed behind thick fog layers of expectations, dreams, and countless unfinished tasks unknown when to complete.

The distance from where you stand to that peak looks overly far off. Rock cliffs appear overly steep, feet begin tiring, and a cold breeze glides past making you shiver.

The familiar voice in your head utters again: *How can I climb up there? I still hold overly many flaws. I will collapse mid-way.*

That suffocating feeling is not because you are weak.

It surfaces because you spent overly much time gazing in a single direction: looking up.

We live in a society perpetually teaching us to face forward, perpetually looking up to fresh heights. Advised never to feel content, perpetually reaching for subsequent peaks.

And in that muddle of gazing up, we inadvertently forget a very vital movement to keep our soul composed.

That is pausing, turning around, and gazing down at the mountain base.

---

## Height Illusions and Suffocation Sensations

Standing at mountain bases gazing up at peaks, spatial structure creates an exceedingly heavy visual and psychological illusion.

The peak looks as if collapsing onto your head. Every slope angle multiplies two-fold. Distance seems stretching endlessly.

The human mind facing overly large work volumes or goals automatically falls into overload states. It begins drawing series of terrible scenarios: failure, collapse, exhaustion, world contempt.

*I hold insufficient skills yet. Insufficient finances yet. Un-ready yet.*

How you view mountains decides your feeling about yourself.

Looking merely at unclimbed mountain parts, you define oneself via what you *lack yet*. You become a perpetual deficiency holder.

Even climbing a thousand meters, looking merely at remaining two thousand meters, you still feel having accomplished nothing. Viewing yourself still as a low-standing person.

Why so many touching achievements others dream of still feel empty and insecure. Never permitting themselves to enjoy conquered heights, because eyes immediately glued to subsequent peaks.

That gazing-up race holds no finish line. And it drains full living joy on each footstep.

---

## Art of Looking Down Is No Self-Complacency

Many fear looking down thinking it a self-complacency sign. Fearing looking back at accomplished items, losing motivation to step forward.

Yet a vast difference exists between self-complacency and sincere recognition.

Self-complacency is standing at some height, beating chest proudly and despising those standing below.

While sincere recognition is turning back to look at the path walked through with a gentle smile and deep respect for oneself.

Try turning around and looking down below.

Look, your starting point lies hidden behind far tree lines.

There stands your version three years ago, five years ago — a person once confused, without knowing where to begin, once weeping silently in lonely nights thinking impossible to pass a mishap.

There stand days you thought your life ended. Days carrying a bleeding heart wound yet next morning still needing to wake up and go to work.

Yet look where you stand today?

Standing right here. Vastly higher than starting points.

The path behind you got paved with no red carpets. Woven from persistent footsteps in rainstorms, from times stumbling with scraped skin yet still rising stubbornly to step forward.

Un-turning to look and acknowledge that journey yourself, who will acknowledge it for you?

---

## Proof of Proven Endurance

Looking down serves not for daydreaming, but gathering an invaluable spiritual fuel type: proof of your own endurance.

Your mind holds a habit of forgetting what you passed through, yet remembering very clearly what you fear ahead.

Turning to look down, you remind your mind of a proven reality: *I experienced steep road sections like this. I survived storms appearing to sweep everything away.*

Present-you is no longer the naive person at mountain bases.

You accumulated scars, and alongside scars is maturity. Feet grew sturdier. Breathing learned to modulate better. You learned selecting foot spots to avoid slipping.

All those experiences came from no book-reading or others' advice. They came from your own blood-and-bone experiences on past road sections.

Why then doubt your capacity to step forward?

If capable of walking from mountain bases to here — a road section past-you thought impossible — what makes you believe impossible to walk a bit further?

Upcoming road sections might remain steep, but the person walking today is a vastly stronger and stouter version.

---

## Liberating Self from Mountain Peak Pressures

The most silent truth regarding life's mountains is: Peak is never where you truly live.

Peak is merely a small point, a brief moment. Standing there a few minutes, inhaling thin air, taking a photo, and then you must step down or move to another mountain.

Your full life essentially transpires on mountain slopes.

Transpires in daily footsteps, in sweat drops falling on rock cliffs, in resting pauses by rock ledges to drink a water sip and admire clouds and sky.

Living merely for peak-touching moments, you turn ninety-nine percent of life's journey into meaningless endurance.

Liberate yourself from instant peak-touching pressures.

The peak will remain there. Going nowhere. You need not conquer it today or tomorrow.

The sole thing needing to be done right now is concentrating on the next step.

A single step. Not a hundred steps. Not full remaining distances.

Merely place one foot forward, sturdily and calmly.

And feeling overly exhausted, permit yourself to sit on a rock ledge, back against mountain walls, face facing the vast valley below.

Gaze at the vast space you conquered. Feel breezes blowing through hair.

You traveled a very long distance already. That is a truth no one can strip from you.

---

## Stepping Forward Lightly with a Grateful Heart

Combining viewing paths ahead and cherishing paths behind, your footsteps grow miraculously light.

Walking no longer in fear or rush of a chased person.

Walking with composed confidence of a pilgrim — a pilgrim cherishing each kilometer walked.

Grateful for flat road sections granting rest. Grateful also for steep rock sections polishing your resilience.

And most importantly, grateful for yourself.

Grateful for self for not quitting in darkest days. Grateful for these feet continuing steps, this heart continuing beats and hope.

Tonight, wherever standing on your life mountain — whether near peaks or merely past mountain bases a bit — grant yourself a quiet space to smile.

You are doing vastly better than what you acknowledge oneself.

Look down, take a deep breath, and feel proud of the distance walked through.

---

## Before You Read the Next Article

Tonight, close eyes and imagine your own version from three or five years ago.

If that person stood before you right now and saw all you passed through to reach today, what do you think that person would say to you?
