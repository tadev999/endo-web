# Naming the Exact Emotion You Hold Is Not to Control It — but Because When Called by Its Proper Name, It Grows Less Scary

*Article 81 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

Moments in life occur when a heavy, gloomy sensation unexpectedly surrounds you without advance warning.

Your chest feels constricting, chest compressing a suffocating air block.

Your mind turns insecure, chaotic and frustrated with surrounding items.

When someone asks you: *“What is wrong with you?”*, you merely know to sigh and reply vaguely:

*“I don't know either... I feel so tired... I feel so terrible...”*

*Tired, terrible, stressed, unstable*... those are generic, grey words we routinely use to tag our full complex internal world.

We toss all turmoil, internal tug-of-wars, wordless pain into a large sack named "stress".

And then, un-knowing what exactly rests inside that sack, we fall into panic.

That vague emotional block resembles a shapeless dark monster, continuously swelling up and swallowing your peace.

Your mind strives to fight it, strives to brush it away or seek ways to control it, yet struggling further, you feel increasingly exhausted and deadlocked.

Yet Endogenism philosophy and modern psychological science both point to a miraculous key to untangle this tangled thread.

You need not destroy or chase away that monster.

Sole thing needing to be done right now is turning on attention lamps, looking straight at it and placing down an exact name.

---

## Fear Coming from Invisible Vagueness

Why does an un-named emotion grow so scary and devastate us so fiercely?

Because human brains are designed to perpetually seek safety in clarity.

When a hazard holds no shape, no boundaries, and no definitions... brains automatically trigger warning mechanisms at maximum levels.

Assuming that vague item is an exceedingly grand survival threat.

Recall images of a small child lying in dark rooms at night.

Looking up at walls seeing a giant, bizarre dark shadow swaying in wind. The child screams crying out of fear, thinking it a hideous monster lurking to swallow it.

Yet when mother steps into rooms turning on night lamps...

The child realizes that scary dark shadow essentially is merely its own coat hanging on window pegs.

The monster immediately vanishes. Fear dissolves not because coats got thrown away, but because seen true to its authentic nature.

Your emotions are completely similar.

Calling it merely "terrible" or "stress", you continue staying in dark rooms with giant dark shadows.

Self-creating a thick fog cloud veiling mind, granting fear opportunities to magnify and multiply many-fold.

---

## Naming Is Not to Control or Destroy

One of most common mistakes working with emotions is perpetually approaching them via controller or fighter attitudes.

Wanting to name emotions to immediately crush them, heal them or force them to vanish.

*“Ah, this is anger, I must extinguish it immediately!”*

*“This is fear, I must become strong!”*

That forced attitude merely creates additional resistance and internal conflict.

Endogenism philosophy reminds that: Purpose of exact emotion naming was never to control or alter it.

Naming is an act of sincere recognition and compassion.

Calling an emotion by its proper name, you shift from victim stances swallowed by emotions to mindful observer stances.

Creating a sacred gap between *your intrinsic self* and *the emotional state passing through you*.

You are no longer "anger" — you are the person perceiving present anger.

You are no longer "pain" — you are tolerant space embracing that pain.

And that perceiving gap alone suffices to strip 80% of emotional monsters' threatening power.

---

## Emotional Granularity — Separating Wave Layers

To name accurately what transpires inside, we need to train a skill named Emotional Granularity.

Capacity to distinguish between subtle emotional nuances rather than dumping all into a generic word.

Let's un-peel a few outer shell layers of familiar emotional sacks together.

Feeling a frustrated flame flare up in chest toward colleagues or loved ones...

Pause a few seconds and self-question: *Is this truly pure "anger"?*

Or hidden deep beneath that anger flame is **hurt** due to un-met expectations?

Or **disrespected feelings**, **un-seen and un-treasured feelings**?

Finding yourself sluggish, lying on bed full days un-wishing to do any task...

*Is this "laziness" as you routinely self-curse?*

Or essentially **mental exhaustion** after a long period over-shouldering strength?

Or **failure fear** disguising under procrastination shells, leaving you un-daring to start new projects?

Finding your heart clawing seeing others' success on social media...

*Is that evil "envy"?*

Or **internal desire whispers** reminding that you abandoned your own dreams overly long?

Each emotion carries a distinct message, a distinct color, and a distinct need.

Naming accurately "hurt", you know needing soothing rather than punishment.

Naming accurately "failure fear", you know needing courage stepping small steps rather than self-blame regarding laziness.

## Metaphor of Fog Travelers and Wooden Signposts

Imagine being a traveler walking through an old forest early morning.

Thick fog veils everywhere, leaving you unable to see paths clearly, un-knowing where drop-offs lie, where bogs lie, where safe paths lie.

Fumbling, panicked and walking in trembling anxiety.

Yet fog beginning to dissipate slightly, seeing a wooden signpost standing by road edges...

Signboard clearly marking: *“Ahead 50m is leaning cliffs. Left side is cool streams.”*

Although cliffs remain there, streams remain there, zero scenery deleted...

Yet travelers' minds immediately regain serenity.

Fearing no longer because knowing exactly terrain faced. Knowing where needing slow steps and where resting.

Naming emotions is personally erecting those wooden signposts in your mind forest.

Rending emotions un-vanished immediately, yet granting you a clear internal orientation map.

Vaguely fumbling no longer in insecurity fog.

## Art of Welcoming Emotions Like Visiting Guests

Poet Rumi wrote an immortal poem regarding human souls as a Guest House.

Daily, fresh guests visit: a joy, a sadness, a bitterness, a fleeting awareness...

Endogenism philosophy invites you to practice emotion-naming following hospitable host spirits.

Un-comfortable emotions appearing, rather than locking doors tightly or screaming in fear...

Calmly pull out a rattan chair, inviting visiting emotion guests to sit down.

Gaze deep into guests' eyes uttering questions gentler:

*“Hello friend. Tell me, what is your exact name?”*

Listen to answers uttering from deep internal depths:

*\*I am Loneliness... I am Shame... I am Self-Disappointment...\**

And that name uttered, nod smiling gently telling it:

*“I see you. Thank you for visiting and letting me know your name. Permitted to stay here in this space as long as needed.”*

The second acknowledging guests' presence via its exact name, liberation magic occurs.

That emotion needs no longer smashing furniture, needs no longer roaring or creating physical pain to attract your attention.

It was seen. It was heard. And will automatically soothe down, then calmly depart when message-reporting missions complete.

---

## From Acknowledgment to Peaceful Gaps

Naming emotions accurately is a small action bearing immensely grand transformative strength.

Helping remove harsh labels self-tagged onto yourself.

Realizing you are no "weak" person, merely carrying an un-healed **abandonment fear**.

Not a "hot-tempered" person, merely carrying overly long accumulated **physical fatigue**.

Proper naming brings authentic freedom — freedom coming not from running away or controlling worlds, but coming from understanding and reconciling with yourself.

Today, if an un-comfortable emotional wave surges inside chest...

Rush not to brush it away, nor rush seeking ways to repair it.

Take a deep breath, direct gentle attention back to heart zones, and patiently seek the most exact name for guests knocking on doors.

When name lamps light up, you will realize zero emotions are scary.

All are merely small waves surging then dissolving on internal peace ocean surfaces inside you.
