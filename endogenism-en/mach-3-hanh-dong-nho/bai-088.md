# Language Does Not Merely Describe Reality — It Shapes Reality. You Can See Merely Things You Hold Names to Call

*Article 88 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

A famous fable exists regarding the Himba tribe in Namibia.

In their language, no words exist to distinguish green and blue. Using a single word *zoozu* referring to both colors. Researchers showing a circle containing 11 green boxes and 1 blue box very clear to modern human eyes, Himba people faced great difficulties pointing out that distinct color box. Simply unable to see the difference.

Conversely, their language holds dozens of different words describing very tiny green color shades that our eyes treat as identical. And pointing out distinct green boxes in a flash.

This story proves a deep linguistic hypothesis: **Language is no mere tool describing the world. Language is precisely what limits and shapes the world you can see.**

Lacking a name to call a phenomenon, you face great difficulties recognizing that phenomenon exists — even while occurring inside your own body and mind.

---

## Internal Vocabulary: When Emotions Tagged Wrong

Most of us hold an exceedingly poor "internal vocabulary". Feeling not okay, routine using generic words like: *sad, bored, tired, angry*.

Lacking more specific names, we begin mis-understanding and mis-tagging what transpires inside us:

- Feeling exhausted over-shouldering others' expectations, yet the sole language held is "lazy" or "inadequate". Judging yourself as **weak and lacking discipline**, rather than recognizing experiencing **chronic burnout**.
- Feeling anxious, restless before a large decision because body warning of overload, yet calling it merely "cowardly". Forcing self to "overcome fear" blindly, rather than listening to body's valid **overload signals**.
- Feeling empty after touching a large milestone, yet knowing merely "unexerted sufficiently". Continuing rushing to subsequent goals, rather than recognizing experiencing **conditional worth illusions**.

Tagging wrong, your solutions will also be wrong. Unable to heal a wound if continuing calling it a stain and seeking ways to wipe it clean.

---

## Power of Naming

In ancient myths, knowing "true names" of creatures or forces grants power controlling them. In psychology, this is completely true.

A famous mindfulness principle exists: **"Name it to tame it."**

Naming accurately your internal state, a safe distance gets created between you and that state. Ceasing to be identified with it.

Try looking at language shifts:

- Instead of saying: *"I am a failure"* ➔ Say: *"I am experiencing **imposter syndrome** sensations."*
- Instead of saying: *"I am very lazy"* ➔ Say: *"My body is alerting regarding **mental energy overload**."*
- Instead of saying: *"I have a terrible life"* ➔ Say: *"I am caught in **external recognition** cycles."*

Altering words, altering how you interact with yourself. Guilt and self-judgment lessen, yielding space for curiosity and self-compassion. Ceasing to face off with emotions, beginning working alongside them as valuable info sources.

---

## Enriching "Endogenous" Vocabulary

Endogenism journey is essentially a language re-learning journey. Learning to name most subtle internal experiences previously bypassed or mis-tagged.

Below are core concepts you will continuously encounter and practice in this book:

1. **Unconditional Worth**: Belief that you deserve merely because existing, independent of productivity or achievements.
2. **Fear-Driven Action vs. Foundation-Driven Action**: Distinguishing deep motives behind what you do.
3. **Emotions as Information**: Treating uncomfortable feelings as data needing reading, no problem needing immediate fixing.
4. **Mental Exhaustion**: Brain energy depletion state from absorbing overly much artificial stimuli.

---

## Practice for Today

Next time an unnamed discomfort wells up inside (perhaps restlessness, throat tightness, or heavy thinking sensations):

1. Rush not brushing it away nor rushing onto phones to evade.
2. Pause, close eyes and ask self: **"If this sensation held a very specific name, what would it be?"**
3. Try seeking a name describing accurately its nuance. Is it *overload*, *loneliness in crowds*, *waiting for recognition*, or *fatigue from appearing okay*?
4. Write that name down on paper. Look at it saying to oneself: *"I see you. You are [emotion name]. Permitted to be here."*

Naming your darkness, it loses a part of its power over you. Begin enriching your internal language starting today, word by word.
