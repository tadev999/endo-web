# Smiling at a Stranger Is Not to Receive in Return — but Because You Just Chose Which Person You Wish to Be in That Moment

*Article 86 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

You walk on a crowded street on a cold drizzling afternoon.

Surrounding people rush, lowering heads avoiding each other's glances, submerged in private umbrellas or glowing phone screens.

Then at a street corner, your eyes accidentally touch a stranger's eyes — a janitor sweeping leaves, a tech driver standing waiting for orders under porches, or a pedestrian walking opposite.

In that brief split second, rather than looking elsewhere with familiar indifference, you smile softly.

A gentle, unboastful smile, taking place within a single second.

That stranger might smile back at you, might stay confused without understanding, or even turn away with no reaction.

Yet that matters no longer.

Because the moment granting that smile, its purpose completed immediately. Smiling not to receive world responses in return.

Smiling because in that exact split second, you made a silent choice regarding your essence: choosing to be a warm person amidst a cold space.

---

## Pressure of Expecting Returns

We live in a world operating on transactions.

Expending effort expecting salary returns. Dedicating care expecting affection returns. Offering a greeting expecting an equivalent reply return.

That transactional thinking soaks deep into every internal life nook, leaving us cautious and calculating even with smallest gestures.

*What if I smile and they smile not back? Will I look stupid?*

*Why friendly with someone I never met and will never meet again?*

Those questions reflect rejection fear and our dependence on external feedback.

Doing a good deed yet hanging its value onto others' reactions, you strip away from oneself your own freedom.

Stranger smiling back, feeling happy. Stranger turning away with cold faces, feeling hurt or regretful.

Thus, your joy or peace belongs no longer to you. Mortgaged via a complete stranger's attitude on roads.

---

## Intrinsic Freedom in a Smile

Endogenism views every small action from a completely different angle.

An action's value resides in no external efficiency or reply reactions. Residing precisely where born: inside the performer's mind and character.

Smiling at a stranger without expecting any return, you practice a highest freedom form.

Freedom originating from within (endogenous freedom).

Smiling not because the other is lovely or did something for you. Smiling because choosing to bring gentleness into this moment.

*I need not this world friendly with me first before I treat it kindly. I can be the beginner.*

This is an immensely powerful cognitive reversal.

Ceasing to be passive mirrors merely reflecting surrounding environments' coldness or rush.

Becoming a transmitting source. Self-deciding the atmosphere you wish to create in your surrounding radius.

That smile, therefore, is a gift granted to strangers, yet first and foremost, a gift bestowed upon yourself.

---

## Character Built in Un-Cared-About Moments

We typically think human identity or character expresses via grand decisions, milestone choices.

Yet actually, your true human self shapes clearest in small, unnamed moments, when no one checks, no one evaluates, and no no rewards promised.

Which person do you wish to become standing in a crowded elevator?

Which person do you wish to become walking past someone encountering hardship on roads?

Choosing to become part of common indifference, blending into grey human streams rushing after personal goals.

Or choosing pausing a beat, opening heart slightly, and letting your presence turn into a silent soothing for surrounding space.

*Right now, amidst this doubt-filled world, wish I to add a bit of warmth or a bit of coldness?*

Each time choosing to smile, choosing holding doors for people behind, choosing nodding greetings to guards — placing a vote for the human version you wish to live alongside.

Self-telling yourself: *"However rushing or harsh worlds be, I choose holding my own gentleness."*

And that internal assertion brings a silent yet exceedingly sturdy self-respect.

---

## Silent Depth of a Split-Second Connection

Never knowing what your fleeting smile might mean to a stranger.

Perhaps that stranger experienced their worst day in life. Received sad news, or feeling completely abandoned amidst an unfamiliar city.

Your rushed smile resolves not their difficulties.

Yet dispatches a silent signal through space: *"I see you. You are here, and a human walking through this life like me."*

*Merely a glance, a smile... can it soothe some burden?*

Might yes, might no. Yet that rests not within scope needing your concern.

What matters is keeping your heart door unlocked tightly. Permitting human-to-human connection transcending unfamiliarity and doubt barriers.

In a world where humans turn increasingly separated behind concrete walls and flat screens, an selfless smile is a courageous action.

A reminder that we are all sharing a human journey filled with anxieties and hope.

---

## Practicing Unconditional Kindness

For smiling at strangers to turn into a soul-nourishing experience rather than causing awkward stiffness, approach it naturally and relaxed.

Turn it into no "task" needing completion or social skill needing practice.

Begin via observing surroundings via less hurried eyes.

Sitting on buses, standing waiting for supermarket payments, remove earphones slightly, lift head up and truly see humans surrounding you.

View them not as obstacles blocking your path, but vivid entities shouldering private thoughts.

And eyes accidentally touching someone's eyes, let natural gentleness from within overflow onto your face.

*What if they turn away?*

Smile at that exact turning away. Tolerating their caution, because perhaps unaccustomed to receiving unreasoned warmth from strangers.

Losing nothing. That smile remains complete inside your heart.

---

## Becoming a Sanctuary of Peace

Living with demanding no return spirits, you realize a miracle: falling never into deficiency states.

Persons perpetually demanding external recognition are persons perpetually feeling poor. Resembling beggars begging each smile, each praise to fill internal voids.

While persons actively granting kindness — even a fleeting smile — are persons living from an unending internal source.

Realizing holding sufficient warmth to share, sufficient peace to radiate, and sufficient character unshakeable by world attitudes.

*This smile is mine. This gentleness is mine. No one can strip it, unless I voluntarily let go.*

That is Stream 3's core in this article series: small actions measured not via scale or applause, but measured via internal self-awareness and compassion depth.

Continue stepping forward on life paths with an open heart.

And each time smiling at strangers, silently bless them, then gently return to your own peaceful stream.

---

## Before You Read the Next Article

Today, walking out into streets or passing familiar spaces, try gifting a silent smile to an unknown friend.

Need not wait for them to look back. Simply let that smile bloom on lips like a greeting welcoming their presence on earth.

After that moment, listen gently to sensations inside your chest. Do you feel your heart expanding a bit?
