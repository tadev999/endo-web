# A Bad Version First — Because Something Imperfect but Existing Is Still Better Than Something Perfect but Only in Your Head

*Article 78 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

Have you ever held in your mind a wonderful idea across many months, even years?

It might be a book you wish to write, a personal project you dream to launch, a creative channel you wish to begin, or simply a plan altering living habits.

In your mind, that plan surfaces exceedingly perfect.

Text lines flow smoothly, projects operate seamlessly, every detail arranged neatly and glittering like a magnificent painting.

Yet sitting down before white paper pages or computer screens, intending to place initial strokes or text lines — your hands pause.

An invisible hesitation appears.

*What if writing it down proves not good as I thought? What if the made product looks awkward, naive? Better wait until I prepare more thoroughly.*

And thus you fold notebooks. You turn off computers. Perfect ideas continue unhurriedly resting in your head, safe and never chipped.

We typically delude oneself that procrastination expresses caution, high standards.

Yet courageously looking straight at truth, you will realize: That is perfectionism's largest trap.

A perfectionism subtly stripping away your capacity to create and live truly.

---

## Sweet Illusions in the Mind Room

A reason exists why we like keeping ideas inside our head: Inside the head, everything is exceedingly easy and glittering.

Ideas residing in imagination shoulder no friction with reality. Facing no limitations of words, skill deficits, or resource hurdles.

It is a gorgeous illusion.

Yet however gorgeous illusions be, they hold no entity. Unable to nourish your soul, unable to help you mature, and unable to create any impact on surrounding worlds.

Resembling a castle built on clouds. You can admire it and feel artificial pride over your creative capacity, yet can never step into that castle to shelter.

And the cost of maintaining that cloud castle is action paralysis in reality.

Holding perfect ideas in head longer, distance between your dreams and actual capacity stretches further. You grow terrified of starting, because knowing any real product you make in initial steps will shatter when compared with the magnificent image in mind.

You fall into a desperate loop: Dreaming perfection — Fearing awkwardness — Doing nothing — And continuing to gnaw regret.

---

## Perfectionism's Indirect Arrogance

We typically think perfectionism is a good quality. Proudly saying: *"I am a perfectionist."*

Yet behind that beautiful label, occasionally lies a hidden indirect arrogance.

Perfectionism whispers in your ear: *You are a special person. Your product must be excellent right from initial times. You are permitted to make no naive or poor items.*

You fear making a "bad version" because fearing admitting that: right now, your capacity rests merely at that level.

You fear others' judgment. You fear your own self-judgment.

Yet think gentler a bit: Can anyone on earth make a great work right on the initial try?

An illustrious writer passes through dozens of messy drafts. A talented painter draws thousands of scribbled sketches. A successful entrepreneur passes through series of failed experiments.

Initial awkwardness is no evidence of inadequacy.

It is the mandatory price to pay for any growth. The sole door leading to refinement.

Un-courageously permitting yourself to make a bad version first, you will perpetually hold no opportunity creating a better version later.

---

## The Leap from 0 to 1

In physics and internal life, largest distance is no distance between 1 and 100.

Largest distance is the leap from 0 to 1.

0 represents the world of ideas, invisibility, illusions resting in heads.

1 represents the world of reality, items born, however misshapen and awkward be.

A poorly written paragraph, holding many spelling flaws, yet resting on paper pages — still holds value thousands of times a perfect novel resting in your head.

Because that poorly written paragraph **exists**.

It stepped past boundaries from invisible to visible. Holding entity. You can see it, touch it, sense its flaws — and most importantly, you can edit it.

You cannot edit an idea resting in head. You can edit merely an item actually written, made, spoken.

Accepting creating a "bad version first", you executed a giant leap: Liberating ideas from thinking dungeons and granting them private life in reality.

It might be unattractive yet, not good yet, but it lived.

And from that humble living, true sculpting journeys can begin.

---

## Gentle Liberation from Pressure

Try granting yourself a special permission today: Permitting yourself to make a bad version.

Starting a task via promising oneself: *Today I need merely create a bad version*, a lightened wave immediately washes through your soul.

Pressure vanishes. Judgment fear collapses.

You strain no longer to appear smart or talented. You return to a child playing with wood blocks.

A child building wooden houses worries not whether the house hits architecture standards. It simply places one wood block onto another and smiles seeing its shape surface.

Approaching work via bad-version-first mindsets, your creativity releases completely.

You courageously experiment with fresh text lines. You dare propose strange ideas normally immediately discarded out of error fear.

You work with gentle curiosity: *Let's see how this bad version looks?*

And strangely, in that relaxation and tolerance, truest strokes, heart-touching sentences begin appearing.

Because unchained by perfection fear, your heart holds space to utter its primal voice.

---

## Sculpting on Real Materials

Holding initial bad versions in hand, subsequent work grows gentler and far more interesting.

You face no longer scary white paper pages. You hold materials to work alongside.

Now you play a finishing craftsman role.

You view that bad version via tolerant sculptor eyes. You trim awkward sentences. You edit unreasonable details. You add materials to empty spots.

Day by day, step by step, initial bad versions silently transform.

Stripping away rough misshapen looks to reveal smoother, more refined lines.

And viewing final finished products, you realize a deep lesson: That wonderful product resulted not from a miraculous genius moment.

It resulted from a series of patient working days alongside an initial bad version.

Lacking courage enduring initial day awkwardness, there would never be today's brilliance.

---

## Action Tonight

Tonight, think about what you kept in head overly long fearing inadequate perfection.

It might be a message you wish to send someone fearing awkwardness.

It might be initial pages of a journal you wish to start.

It might be a small task list you wish to do to tidy your life.

Open notebooks, or pick up phones, and place down initial lines.

Whisper softly to yourself: *This is merely a bad version first. And that is completely okay.*

Strive not to render it good. Strive not to render it beautiful.

Merely render it **exist**.

Because an imperfect item present in reality is perpetually better than an absolute perfect item resting forever in your head.
