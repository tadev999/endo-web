# You Are Comparing Your Chapter 1 with Someone Else's Chapter 20 — And Ignoring That Neither Is Your Actual Measurement

*Article 45 / Stream 2: Building Foundations*

---

On certain evenings, you accidentally scroll past an article or video on social media.

In it, someone the same age — or even a few years younger than you — stands at their career peak. They speak at a major conference with glowing confidence, own a smoothly operating business, publish bestselling books, and possess a warm family alongside a relaxed composure.

Looking back at yourself, you sit in a small rented room or cluttered workspace. You struggle with a flawed first draft, worry about next month's rent money, or feel confused about where your career path will go three years from now.

A constricting feeling surfaces in your chest. A melancholic whispering voice echoes in your head: *Why at this age do I still hold nothing in hand? Why have others gone so far, while I remain stuck at the starting line?*

Common advice you typically hear in such situations is: *Don't compare your chapter 1 with someone else's chapter 20.*

That advice is true, yet it walks merely half the distance. It comforts you that you are merely at your journey's initial stage, and if you patiently strive, one day you will also touch your own chapter 20.

Endogenism views this story at a deeper layer and poses a completely different question: *What happens if neither chapter 1 nor chapter 20 is the true measurement of your human worth?*

---

## The Trap of Time Slices

First, inspect the nature of comparison our minds typically fall into daily.

The modern digitized world is an exceptionally sophisticated information filtering machine. It displays only meticulously edited chapter 20s, coated in brilliant color filters and compressed into a few hundred words posts or short video clips.

What you see in someone else's chapter 20 is the final output. You see not their preceding nineteen chapters: sleepless nights due to deadlocks, bitter failures un-dared mentioned to anyone, harsh rejections, solitude on long roads, and context luck they themselves couldn't control.

Meanwhile, regarding yourself, you know every single detail of chapter 1. You know internal doubts, un-fixed bad habits, petty fears, and your own postponements.

Comparing your own sticky backstage full panorama with someone else's elaborately polished ad film is a game where failure is guaranteed before starting.

The human mind easily falls into this trap because it tends to flatten reality. It turns life into a straight sprint track, where everyone starts from a single point and heads toward a shared finish line.

Yet life was never an athletics track. It is a vast forest, where each organism bears a different gene set, grows in different soil types, and possesses a completely unique growth rhythm.

---

## The Deeper Layer: Reliance on Chapter Structure

If merely stopping at reminding yourself *be patient waiting for chapter 20*, you remain trapped in the same mindset frame of reference.

You still implicitly acknowledge that: present (chapter 1) is a deficient, flawed state needing rapid passage; while future (chapter 20) is where true worth, recognition, and peace reside.

This thinking mode turns your life's entire present phase into a waiting period. You live in a "postponed living" state. You think you truly begin living only upon reaching that position, that income level, or that degree.

Yet what will occur when you actually touch the chapter 20 you once craved?

Psychological reality shows, if you don't feel complete at chapter 1, you won't feel complete at chapter 20. Upon reaching old goals, the mind immediately defines that as "new normal state", and continues forging another person's chapter 40 or 50 to begin a fresh comparison loop.

Chapter 1 is no penalty. Chapter 20 is no paradise either.

Both are merely external time slices, representing different circumstances, skills, and work scales. They carry zero power deciding whether you are a valuable human being.

---

## Metaphor of the Wrong-Dimension Ruler

Imagine holding a temperature ruler to measure an art painting's length.

You place the ruler on the drawing canvas, read the indicator thirty-seven degrees Celsius, then feel disappointed because the painting "is merely thirty-seven long". That disappointment originates not from the painting itself, but from using the wrong measurement tool from the start.

Society hands us very clear extrinsic rulers: bank account money, business card titles, follower numbers, house scales, or marriage and child-bearing ages.

Those rulers are designed to measure economic efficiency and social order. They hold their roles in operating a large system. But they are completely useless when used to measure a human's internal quality.

When using extrinsic rulers to measure your life, you fall into two dangerous extremes.

If ruler indicator is low (you are at chapter 1), you feel inferior, self-deprecating, and view yourself as a failure. You treat yourself with internal struggling and punishment.

If ruler indicator is high (you touched chapter 20 by social standards), you easily fall into illusory arrogance or anxious trembling. You constantly dread one day the ruler indicator lowers and you lose worth in public eyes.

Both extremes remove you from internal foundations. You turn yourself into a slave to the ruler handed to you by others.

---

## Where Is the Actual Measurement?

So if neither chapter 1 nor chapter 20 is the actual measurement, where does a human's true measurement lie?

It lies in items impossible to package into a social media post, impossible to print on a business card, and no algorithm can measure.

The actual measurement lies in **internal honesty**: Do you dare look straight at your emotions, fears, and limits without deceit or blaming others?

The actual measurement lies in **presence capacity**: When sitting eating a meal with loved ones, when listening to a suffering friend, when walking under tree rows — is your mind truly there, or torn apart by future anxieties?

The actual measurement lies in **silent kindness**: How you treat those bringing you zero benefit, how you react to yourself when making mistakes, and the tolerance you hold for surrounding world breakdowns.

The actual measurement lies in **harmony with core values**: Are you living true to what you believe, even when that means walking slower than others or relinquishing glittering opportunities contrary to personal ethics?

A person at chapter 1 — holding no large external achievements yet living kindly, present deeply, and honest with self — possesses an actual measurement far larger than someone at a brilliant chapter 20 whose inside is a scary void and mind flooded with manipulation and insecurity.

---

## Returning to Intrinsic Rhythm

Upon realizing this, a massive pressure weight gets lifted off your shoulders.

You no longer need to hurriedly burn stages jumping from chapter 1 to chapter 20. You realize chapter 1 holds unique beauty and lessons chapter 20 can never possess.

Chapter 1 is the time of trial freedom, humble learning, and silent accumulations no one notices. That is when your root system is plunging deep into soil darkness — a quiet process deciding the stability of the entire future oak tree.

If continuously glancing at neighboring trees already flowering and fruiting to chastise your root system why it hasn't bloomed, you are harming your own living process.

Ask yourself: *Today, am I living completely with my present book chapter?*

Living completely with chapter 1 means writing that draft with full attentiveness, despite knowing it's imperfect. It means learning patience lessons, accepting initial clumsiness, and smiling at toddler steps.

When mastering chapter 1 from internal foundations, chapter 2 will automatically open naturally. Not because you won the comparison race with others, but because your growth is an irresistible stream flowing from within.

---

## Stepping Off the Extrinsic Ruler Race

Next time, catching yourself squinting at someone's chapter 20 on your phone screen feeling your heart sink, perform a small pause action.

Smile at that person. Acknowledge that they walked a long journey to reach there, and congratulate what they achieved. Sincere congratulations liberate you from envy's toxic energy.

And immediately after, return to yourself. Place a hand on your chest and remind yourself:

*That is their book chapter, written with their ruler. And this is my book page.*

*I am not here to win anyone else's race. I am here to live most completely and deeply with what is present in my hand.*

Your worth was never in the chapter number you walked past. Your worth lies in the presence quality you bring into each page you are writing right now.
