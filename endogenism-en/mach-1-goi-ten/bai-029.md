# When Love and Control Arrive at the Same Time — And You Don't Know If You Are Allowed to Feel Both

*Article 29 / Stream 1: Naming*

---

There is a type of relationship exceedingly hard to speak about — not because it is rare, but because it is complex in ways everyday language lacks vocabulary to articulate.

Not a bad relationship overall. Not that the other person is bad. No violence, no intentional coldness, nothing you can point toward stating "here is what is wrong." Simply — simultaneously, from the identical person, you receive genuine love and genuine control. And you don't know if you are allowed to feel both.

Not allowed to feel sad, since they love you. Not allowed to feel angry, since they are doing the best they can. Not allowed to feel suffocated, since that appears ungrateful. And not allowed to speak it, since speaking it would hurt the person you love.

I spent years sitting with that feeling, not knowing what to call it — knowing only that it felt heavier when holding back and more complex when trying to speak it out.

---

## Why Love and Control Can Coexist

A common misunderstanding exists — nurtured by how we typically imagine love — that love and control cannot coexist. That if someone genuinely loves you, they won't control you. And conversely, if they control you, that love isn't real.

Reality is far more complex.

Control does not always stem from malice or lack of care. Vast amounts of control within close relationships — particularly in families — stem from anxiety. From fear of losing the loved one, fear of them making mistakes, fear of uncontrollable events occurring. Control, in such instances, is how one handles anxiety — not how one exercises dominance.

And when control stems from anxiety rather than malice, it typically accompanies genuine love. The person controlling you can genuinely love you. The pain you feel is real. Their love is also real. Neither is false — both exist simultaneously.

And that is why it is so complex to navigate.

---

## What This Combination Can Look Like

Because "love and control simultaneously" is not something immediately identifiable, looking at specific forms in which it surfaces can be helpful.

**Anxiety expressed as surveillance.** Asking where you are, what you are doing, with whom — not to dominate, but out of genuine worry. Yet even stemming from worry, that surveillance generates the feeling of being untrusted, not permitted to be an adult, not permitted private space.

**Love expressed through sacrifice — and sacrifice generating pressure.** When someone sacrifices greatly for you and you know it, refraining from feeling obligated to repay by satisfying their expectations is exceptionally difficult. No one states it directly. But it hangs in the air — like an invisible debt you don't recall agreeing to borrow.

**Care expressed through making decisions for you.** Not to control, but because they believe they know what is best for you. Yet the outcome, however good the intention, remains: you are not permitted to learn to decide for yourself, not permitted to make your own mistakes, not permitted to develop capacity to trust your own judgment.

**Affection withdrawn when you fail to follow their wishes.** This is the most subtle form and frequently inflicts the deepest wounds — not because anyone intentionally punishes, but because the anxious and disappointed person expresses that emotion through silence, through cold posture, through lacking customary warmth. And you learn that to be loved, you must comply. To be safe, you must obey.

---

## What You Are Permitted to Feel

Here is what many in this relationship need to hear — and rarely hear clearly enough:

You are permitted to love them and still feel suffocated by them.

You are permitted to be grateful for what they did and still feel angry at how they did it.

You are permitted to comprehend that they are doing the best they can and still feel that their best is inflicting hurt upon you.

You are permitted to love them and still require distance.

You are permitted to hold all of those simultaneously — without needing to choose one over the other, without needing to simplify everything to render it easier to process.

Emotions do not operate by binary rules — not "if you love, you can't be angry", not "if you are grateful, you can't feel hurt." Both can be true right now — and both deserve acknowledgment, not choosing one to feel the other.

---

## Distance Is Not Betrayal

Something many in this situation do — and frequently feel guilty for doing — is creating distance. Whether distance in geography, distance in degree of sharing, or distance in letting the other person influence decisions.

And typically accompanying that distance is guilt — that creating distance is abandoning the one who loves you, being ungrateful, being selfish, betraying them.

Yet distance is not betrayal. Distance, when created not out of hatred but out of necessity, is an entirely logical act of self-preservation. And occasionally, distance is the sole mechanism permitting the relationship to exist in a healthier manner long-term — because when no longer feeling suffocated, you can genuinely be present in that relationship, rather than merely enduring it.

That does not mean distance is always the answer, or that it carries no price. It does. And occasionally creating distance leads to difficult conversations, misunderstandings, or even losing a piece of the relationship. Yet living without distance when needing it also carries a price — and that price is typically yourself.

---

## Not About Blaming

It must be stated clearly once more, because this matters to prevent misunderstanding: the goal is not placing blame on the one loving you in this manner.

They frequently do not know that what they are doing is controlling. They do not know that their love, though real, generates unhealthy pressure. They are doing the best they can with the tools they possess — and those tools, frequently learned from their environment, are not always the best tools.

The goal is not placing blame. The goal is seeing clearly — not by denying their love, and not by denying the impact of that control. Holding both simultaneously, with honesty and without judgment, is the hardest task — and also the most necessary step to begin finding a way forward.

---

## Before You Read the Next Article

If you recognize yourself in this article — permit yourself to sit with that a moment prior to striving to resolve anything.

No need to act immediately. No need to decide anything immediately. Simply ask:

*What emotions am I carrying that I haven't permitted myself to feel fully — because they seem contradictory, or because I didn't know if I had the right to feel them?*

Permitting them to exist — all of them, simultaneously — is the initial step.
