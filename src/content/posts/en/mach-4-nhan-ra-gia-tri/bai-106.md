# What is wealth, if health, peace, and freedom aren't part of your definition?

*Article 106 / Stream 4: Recognizing Value*

---

Have you ever woken up at 4 a.m., hearing your own heart pounding in your chest because of some invisible anxiety about the future or about money?

You reach for the smartphone on your nightstand. The screen lights up, showing your bank balance, stock notifications, or work messages lined up waiting for you.

From the outside world's perspective, you might be a model of success. You have a job many people dream of, you wear expensive clothes, and you own the assets society uses to measure "wealth."

But right in that 4 a.m. moment, facing yourself in the dark, a cold question crawls up into your mind.

*Am I actually wealthy at all?*

If wealth is defined by the number in your bank account, but the price paid is a body in a constant state of exhaustion, a mind that has never known a peaceful day, and a life completely stripped of the freedom to choose — then what does that wealth even mean?

We're living in an age where the definition of wealth has been cruelly narrowed down to numbers and material symbols.

We throw ourselves relentlessly into the race of accumulating visible things, only to go unknowingly bankrupt in the things that truly nourish life from within.

It's time we stopped, took a deep breath, and rewrote the definition of wealth for our own lives.

---

## The Price of One-Sided Wealth

Society teaches us to worship material accumulation as the ultimate measure of success.

We admire people who work 80 hours a week, people who are always busy, people who own real estate or luxury cars. We treat that as proof of a "winning" life.

But rarely do we see the dark voids hiding behind that perfect picture.

There's quietly deteriorating health: chronic stomach pain from stress, restless sleep dependent on medication, and an immune system worn down by constant pressure.

There's a mind bankrupt of peace: living in a constant state of anxiety, afraid of being left behind, unable to fully enjoy any moment because the mind is always calculating the next step.

And there's the complete loss of personal freedom: you no longer own your own time — your time has been sold entirely to work, to other people's expectations, and to maintaining an expensive lifestyle you've unwittingly tied yourself to.

*There's a terrifying kind of poverty: poverty of time, poverty of peace of mind, and poverty of presence with your own life...*

When you trade your health, peace, and freedom for material wealth, you're making the worst-losing transaction of your life.

Because gold and fame can never buy back a healthy stomach, a dreamless good night's sleep, or a leisurely afternoon watching the rain fall without feeling guilty about it.

---

## Health — The Only Foundation of All Wealth

In Endogenism, health is not a means to work more efficiently. Health is the first and most original form of wealth.

Consider this for a moment: when you have a healthy body, you hold a thousand different wishes and dreams. You want to travel, to create, to love, to experience the world.

But when your body falls ill, that entire complex world immediately shrinks down to a single wish: how to stop the pain.

A resilient body, deep and calm breath, clear eyes, and a sharp mind — these are priceless assets no billionaire can buy back once they've already lost them.

Health is where your entire life experience on this earth is stored.

When you respect your own health — by knowing when to stop when you're tired, by listening to your body's warning signals, by eating a healthy meal with full attention — you're accumulating the most precious capital of your life.

Someone with a healthy body and a clear mind is already far wealthier than anyone forced to lie in pain from illness on a gold-plated bed.
