# Whose definition of "success" is this — and do you actually agree with it?

*Article 99 / Stream 4: Recognizing Value*

---

We are living in a world that comes with ready-made answers.

Since our school days, we've been handed a pre-built blueprint of a successful life: get into a prestigious university, find a stable job with a high salary, buy a house, buy a car, get married, have children, and climb up into higher management positions.

This blueprint is advertised everywhere: in family stories, in the news, in advertisements, and on social media feeds.

We accept that blueprint as a given, without ever questioning it. We throw ourselves into the race, give it everything we have, and accept trading away our health, time, and peace of mind to meet those criteria.

But there's an important question very few people ask themselves before setting out: **"Whose definition of success is this, really? And do I actually agree with it?"**

---

## The Trap of a Borrowed Definition

When you chase a definition of success borrowed from other people or from society, you're playing a game that's very hard to win. And even when you win, you still don't get real happiness.

This phenomenon is called **"empty success."**

It happens when you achieve everything society calls an accomplishment — a good title, a high income, a beautiful house — yet when you come home at night, you still feel empty and alone. You look in the mirror and ask yourself: *"I have everything, so why do I still not feel okay?"*

The reason is simple: you climbed a very tall ladder, only to realize it was leaning against the wrong wall.

Society's default definition of success is usually based on **external accumulation** (money, fame, status, recognition). But the deepest human need belongs to **inner wholeness** (freedom, genuine connection, meaning, and inner peace).

When you try to use external values to fill inner emptiness, it's like trying to quench your thirst by drinking salt water. The more you drink, the thirstier you become.

---

## Designing Your Own Measure for Your Own Life

The first step to recognizing your own worth is reclaiming the right to define your own life. You need to shift from the question *"How do I succeed?"* to the question *"What does success look like for me?"*

Every person has a completely different core value system. For you:
- Success might not mean becoming a top executive, but **having enough free time** to play with your children every afternoon.
- Success might not mean owning a mansion, but **doing creative work** that makes you excited to wake up every morning.
- Success might not mean being famous, but **having a small circle of genuine friends** where you can be yourself without hiding anything.

No definition is nobler than another. The right definition is the one that gives you a deep sense of aliveness and peace from within.

When you dare to write down your own measure, you'll no longer feel pressure to compare yourself to or compete with others. You'll realize they're running on their own track, toward their own finish line. And you'll be walking calmly on your own path, savoring every step of it.

---

## A Practice for Today

Take a blank sheet of paper and do a small exercise that requires absolute honesty:

1. Draw a line dividing the paper into two columns.
2. In the left column, write: **"What society says I must achieve to be considered successful"** (for example: title, income, car, fame...).
3. In the right column, write: **"The moments in the past year when I felt truly alive, happy, and at peace"** (for example: afternoon walks, a deep conversation, the feeling of solving a difficult problem...).
4. Observe the difference between the two columns. Are you spending 90% of your energy chasing the left column while what you actually need lies in the right one?

It's time you allowed yourself to drop the borrowed definition and start living by your own measure. This life is yours, and you are the only one responsible for its happiness.
