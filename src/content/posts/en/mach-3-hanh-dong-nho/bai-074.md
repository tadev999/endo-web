# Five Book Pages Daily Is Not to Become a Heavy Reader — but to Dedicate 15 Minutes Accompanying Another's Thinking

*Article 74 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

Have you ever looked up at your bookshelf and felt a subtle wave of guilt rise?

Spines with unfolded creases, unopened pages standing silent in line under lamps. They resemble silent reminders regarding unfinished goals: *This year I must finish reading fifty books. I must become a heavy reader, broadly informed.*

And thus, under that metric burden, you open a book with a rushed feeling. You count each page, calculate each minute, striving to read fast to cross a line off to-do lists.

Reading that way, do you feel yourself truly absorbing anything? Or closing pages, words immediately float away, leaving an exhausted void?

We inadvertently turned reading into a consumption race. We measure knowledge via book quantities experienced, rather than empathy depth accumulated.

Yet if returning reading to its most primal nature?

Five book pages daily is no self-optimization formula. It exists not to turn you into a scholar or heavy reader.

It is simply fifteen minutes you permit yourself to pause your own race, to walk silently beside another's thinking.

---

## When Knowledge Transforms into Consumption Achievements

Modern culture teaches us to view everything through performance lenses.

We hear advice on reading books at high speed, summarizing a book in ten minutes, consuming maximum info in shortest time.

Books turn into a spiritual commodity. Reading many books turns into a medal proving to the world we are disciplined and self-developing.

*How many books did I read this month?*

That question inadvertently pushes you into a perpetual anxiety state. Reading books while eyes continuously glance at remaining page counts. Impatient encountering a long paragraph describing emotions or scenery. Wanting merely to extract "core lessons" rapidly to jump to the next book.

And in that desperate race, what gets forgotten is the writer's soul — and your own soul as well.

A book was written not to become a metric on achievement boards. It is the crystallization of years of contemplation, sleepless nights, pain and deep personal discoveries distilled word by word for life.

Reading hurriedly merely to count quantities, you resemble a tourist riding horses looking at flowers, passing a brilliant garden without once pausing to inhale a single flower's fragrance.

You collected garden photos, yet were never truly inside that garden.

---

## Fifteen Minutes Leaving the Solitary Mind Room

Each among us, willingly or not, lives in one's own mind room.

In that room, surrounded by familiar thoughts, fixed prejudices, repeating anxieties. Viewing the world through windows of modest personal life experiences.

And occasionally, that room grows exceedingly suffocating.

*Why does no one understand me? Why does the world operate so strangely like this? How to escape this deadlock?*

Opening a book and quietly reading five pages, you execute a courageous action: opening your room door and stepping into another's mind room.

In those brief fifteen minutes, you temporarily shed your heavy "ego". You are no longer a person shouldering daily life pressures.

You become a silent companion.

Viewing the world through eyes of a writer living in past centuries. Sensing pain through the heart of a philosopher having tasted ups and downs. Observing the universe through a curious scientist's lens.

Five book pages demand not resolving their problems. It merely invites your presence there, listening to them speak.

Those are fifteen minutes liberated from ego solitude. A gentle pause rhythm for your mind to breathe another thinking's air.

---

## Silent Conversation Transcending Time

A quiet wonder exists in reading books that other entertainment forms cannot replace.

Watching a movie or scrolling a video, info gets bombarded onto you passively. Images and sounds fill every void, allowing your mind no space to respond.

Yet reading book pages, that is a silent two-way dialogue.

The writer places down text lines, yet your imagination and life experience breathe soul into those text lines.

*Seems this person speaks of the exact feeling I experienced at 20...*

*Oh, turns out a gentler way to view failure exists...*

In quiet gaps between text lines, you meet the author — and strangely, meet yourself again.

A good book is no book supplying full answers. It is a book creating a sufficiently safe space for you to pose to oneself questions for your life.

And you need not read full long chapters to acquire that moment. Occasionally, merely a sentence line in five short pages suffices to touch a secret nook concealed long ago.

That is why five book pages suffice.

Sufficing small so you feel no pressure. And sufficing deep to sow in your heart a contemplative seed for a all day long.

---

## Not Memorizing — but Allowing Collisions to Bloom

Many fear reading merely five pages daily, forgetting what got read on previous pages. Fearing un-"memorizing" knowledge.

Yet ask yourself: is reading's purpose truly memorizing each detail?

Recall meals eaten across a full past year. Remember exactly what you ate on Tuesday three months ago?

Surely not. Yet those foods nourished your body, becoming part of your bones, flesh and blood today.

Books are similar.

Five book pages read today need not be memorized for test-taking. Need not take careful notes of main points if that tires you out.

What matters will automatically stay.

A living attitude, a tolerant perspective, a beautiful word, a quiet understanding — soaking through your consciousness naturally. Entering how you think, how you behave toward others, how you face difficulties.

You read books not accumulating knowledge like stowing goods in warehouses.

You read books for your thinking to be continuously collided, polished and expanded by other thinkings.

And that expansion process transpires exceedingly silently. Demanding no rush. Demanding merely regularity and sincerity.

---

## Fifteen Kind Minutes Dedicated to Yourself

Ultimately, dedicating fifteen minutes to read five book pages daily is a very gentle self-care form.

In a long day filled with phone notification noise, work expectations, external world demands — those fifteen minutes are your private holy land.

That is when turning off noise. Brewing tea, sitting down in a quiet corner, and opening book pages.

Zero graders. Zero chasing deadlines. Zero algorithms striving to attract your attention to sell goods.

Only you, text lines, and a wonderful thinking awaiting your company.

*Today I need not conquer the world. I need merely walk five book pages with this person.*

Approaching reading with that mindset, guilt vanishes. Pressure of long unread book lists vanishes.

You read no longer to become a "heavy reader". You read because cherishing moments slowed down, heart expanded to the world.

And strangely, unpressured to read much, you crave opening books daily.

Because those five book pages cease being a chore. It became a loyal companion, awaiting you at familiar desk corners after daily fatigue.

---

## Looking Back at Companionship Through Years

Accompanying five book pages daily, after a year, you walked alongside thousands of pages of diverse thinkings.

Lived through hundreds of lives, walked through countless lands of emotion and knowledge.

Yet what matters most is not page counts walked through.

What matters most is today's you became a better listener. A person no longer easily judging contrasting perspectives, because knowing behind each perspective lies a deep worldview.

You grow gentler with yourself and surrounding people.

Because learning how to patiently walk page by page, unhurriedly, demanding no instant outputs.

That is the silent strength of small actions maintained via love rather than dry discipline burdens.

Tonight, open the book resting on the desk.

Worry not about when to finish. Care not how many chapters long this book is.

Merely read five pages. And feel the warmth when another soul quietly converses with you in the still night.

## Before You Read the Next Article

Look at the book nearest you right now.

Opening it and quietly reading a few pages tonight, are you ready to shelve "finishing" wishes in exchange for fifteen minutes truly present and listening to the writer's thinking?
