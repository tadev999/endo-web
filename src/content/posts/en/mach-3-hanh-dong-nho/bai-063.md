# Dopamine and True Nourishment — How to Distinguish What Is Stimulating You from What Is Truly Nourishing You?

*Article 63 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

A painful paradox exists in our modern life.

Never in human history could humans access multiple entertainment forms, delicious food, and convenient vehicles so easily. Merely with a soft swipe on flat screens, you can watch thousands of funny videos, purchase any item you like, or order hundreds of dishes delivered right to doors.

We live in an ocean flooded with instant joys.

Yet also never did humans feel exhausted, emotionally drained, and empty to this degree.

After hours of scrolling social media unstopping, you feel by no means full of energy or wiser. You feel tired eyes, dazed mind, and an invisible gloom weighing on the chest.

After finishing an impulsive meal high in sugar and fats, initial excitement rapidly makes way for heavy, guilty, and sluggish sensations.

This sixty-third article appears to help you look straight at that hidden psychological mechanism.

It helps you distinguish clearly between two items frequently equated in consumer society: what is stimulating you and what is truly nourishing you.

---

## Dopamine Addiction and Craving Illusion

Neurobiologically, dopamine is typically called the "pleasure hormone". But that is a huge misunderstanding.

Dopamine is no hormone of happiness or satisfaction sensations. Dopamine is the hormone of **craving** and **seeking**.

Dopamine's evolutionary duty is pushing you to act to seek rewards: food, mates, or safety. Seeing a new phone notification, an enticing video title, or a discounted item, your brain emits a dopamine surge.

That dopamine surge brings you no joy. It generates a mild tension state — an irresistible urge: *Click it! Watch it! Buy it!*

And executing that behavior, dopamine drops immediately. You fall into a disappointed state needing a fresh, stronger stimulation dose to maintain temporary comfortable sensations.

That is precisely stimulation's endless loop: Craving — Stimulation — Drop — Craving More.

Living completely under dopamine mechanism rule, you resemble a person drinking seawater to quench thirst. The more you drink, the thirstier you feel. The more you scroll, the lonelier you feel. The more you shop, the emptier you feel.

*Why having all these items do I still feel no peace?*

Because you supply your soul short-term stimulants, not long-term nourishment.

---

## True Nourishment Nature

If dopamine represents stimulation and craving, true nourishment links to completely different biological substances — typically Serotonin and Oxytocin.

Serotonin is the hormone of contentment and presence. It says not: *Seek more!* It softly whispers: *Everything right now is enough. You are here, and you are safe.*

True nourishment is all that renders your internal energy full, deep, and tranquil after experiencing.

A wholesome meal cooked with attentiveness generates no exploding sugar shock, yet brings warm, comfortable sensations lasting hours in the stomach.

A deep book offers you no instant hearty laughs, yet leaves in your heart lingering reflective echoes, helping you comprehend self and life better.

A sincere conversation with a soulmate holds no sensational plot points, yet fills lonely voids and leaves your heart feeling understood.

True nourishment is non-addictive. It forces you not to consume more.

Conversely, it liberates you from dependence on external factors, returning you to original self-sufficiency and peace.

---

## Metaphors of the Straw Fire and Glowing Embers

To comprehend this difference visually, contemplate straw fire and glowing ember images in a cold winter night.

Dopamine stimulants resemble tossing a bundle of dry straw into a fireplace.

Dry straw flares up immediately with crackling sparks, pleasant popping sounds, and towering flames illuminating room corners. In the initial ten seconds, it creates a fierce heat source illusion.

Yet in a brief flash, dry straw burns out completely. The flame snaps off, returning the room to darkness and biting cold. And you must hastily seek another straw bundle to toss in.

True nourishment resembles glowing embers kindled from solid ironwood or oak logs.

Glowing embers flare not fiercely. They generate no loud popping noises or towering display flames.

They merely glow red silently, quietly holding heat beneath a thin ash layer.

Yet those glowing embers hold capacity to radiate gentle, enduring heat throughout long nights. It warms the room, heats tea kettles, and brings absolute peace of mind to those seated by the fire.

Is your present life illuminated by fleeting straw fires, or warmed by enduring glowing embers?

## Clear Self-Questioning Before All Choices

How do you distinguish if an upcoming action is a stimulant or nourishment, when boundaries between them are occasionally very fragile?

The answer lies in your honest questioning oneself stance right before starting.

Form the habit of creating a three-second pause and asking yourself:

*Am I doing this because it truly nourishes me, or am I striving to escape present emptiness and fatigue?*

Reaching for your phone: *Am I opening the phone seeking needed info, or fearing facing the room's silence?*

Intending to open the fridge seeking snacks at ten at night: *Is my body truly hungry for food, or is my mind hungry for love and rest?*

Intending to plunge into a fierce online debate: *Am I uttering this to spread understanding, or craving winning feelings to soothe a wounded ego?*

These questions serve not to chastise oneself or judge yourself. They appear like headlights illuminating unconscious habit darkness.

The moment realizing you seek stimulants, you reclaim choice rights. You are no longer an automatic slave to the dopamine loop.

---

## Micro Nourishment Rituals

You need not abandon full technology, enter deep forests, or live a harsh ascetic life to escape dopamine manipulation.

Instead, in Stream 3, we learn to quietly insert into daily life "micro nourishment rituals" — small actions holding capacity to accumulate serotonin and peace.

Drinking a warm water sip in silence in the morning, feeling cool water stream going down the throat.

Spending five minutes standing by windows admiring a green sprout stretching in sunshine, listening to bird songs outside porches.

Opening a deep philosophy or literature book page, reading slowly each line and permitting the mind to contemplate unhurriedly.

Walking slowly on the ground, sensing earth's sturdy support under feet.

Hugging a loved one or pet five full seconds, without thinking, unhurriedly releasing.

These actions seem to create no sensational excitement. They spike not your dopamine.

Yet they accumulate bit by bit internal stability. They remind your body and mind that: Life lies not in external displays, but in internal sensing depth.

---

## Returning Contentment to Each Moment

The largest shift moving from "stimulation" to "nourishment" lies in how you experience time.

Dominated by dopamine, you live perpetually in the future tense. You feel present moments inadequate, and happiness lies in the next video, next item, or next achievement.

Nourished by presence, you return to the present tense.

You realize right now, sitting here, breathing a peaceful breath, no danger threatens you. Life bestowed upon you sufficient basic conditions to feel grateful.

You need not flee yourself further. You need not fill every silent void with social media noise.

True nourishment brings you an invaluable freedom type: Freedom from manipulation by artificial cravings created by external worlds.

Be gentle with yourself on this journey. No one can eliminate dopamine completely from life, nor do we need to do so.

Merely choose daily to replace a shallow stimulation behavior with a deep nourishment behavior.

A small action. A kind choice.

And you will see your soul gradually stabilize again, like a serene lake surface after wild waves pass.
