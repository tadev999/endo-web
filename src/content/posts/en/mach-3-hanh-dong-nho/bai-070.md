# A Kind Message Sent — and You Just Made Someone Feel Seen

*Article 70 / Stream 3: Small Actions*

---

Each day, our hands glide past hundreds of notifications and send out dozens of messages.

We message to assign work, message to ask about schedules, message to forward news links or drop a quick emoji into group chats.

Never in human history became connection so easy and dense.

Yet, a sad paradox unfolds: amidst an ocean of messages sent every second, humans feel lonelier and more invisible than ever. We chat a great deal, yet rarely feel truly listened to or understood.

Human soul's largest need is not receiving surface attention or soulless heart likes.

Our deepest need is the sensation of "being seen" — having someone recognize one's existence, silent efforts, and unique values amidst this rushing world.

This article serves as a warm suggestion: spend two minutes sending a sincere kind message to someone. For no work purpose, but merely to let them know: they are being seen.

---

## Surface Connection and Silent Loneliness

Think about people surrounding you: the colleague sitting at the opposite desk, a long-unseen close friend, or your own parents.

Each among them shoulders a private battle that the external world knows nothing about.

Someone is silently facing financial anxieties. Someone is exhausted over unshareable family responsibilities. Someone is struggling with self-worth doubts, asking if anyone recognizes their daily efforts.

We typically conceal those wounds and fatigue behind a calm face or a social smile.

*Perhaps everyone among us experienced days feeling completely invisible, standing amidst a crowd...*

Lacking anyone recognizing or touching that truth, loneliness slowly accumulates. We begin believing we matter not, that if vanishing, life's stream alters nothing.

An unexpected kind message sent in that moment resembles a candle lit in dark night. It tears apart invisible feelings, bringing instant warmth to the recipient.

---

## A Non-Transactional Message

What creates this small action's power lies in its "non-transactional" nature.

In everyday life, most of our communications carry some purpose: asking a work favor, demanding information, or maintaining a beneficial relationship.

A true kind message is a message demanding no reply or privilege.

It is no generic social phrase like "Wishing you a happy new week!" or a formal check-in question. It is a specific, personal recognition originating from your sincere observation.

That can be a message sent to a colleague: *"In the meeting earlier, I saw you were very patient explaining issues to everyone despite the tense situation. I truly respect your composure."*

That can be a message sent to an old friend: *"Today I unexpectedly recalled our project work last year. Thank you for perpetually standing by encouraging me then."*

Or simply sent to relatives: *"I just thought about the meals mom cooked. Working hard lately I see how hard mom labored. I love you."*

Those lines cost you no more than two minutes to write. But for the recipient, that might be the sole thing holding them from collapsing on a terrible day.

---

## Power of Recognizing Silent Efforts

We humans tend to praise each other merely when someone achieves a brilliant milestone: a promotion, a wedding, or a large award.

Yet those brilliant moments are rare. Life consists mostly of normal days and unknown silent efforts.

Precisely in those normal days, recognition grows priceless.

Noticing a small detail another did — keeping a polite stance with waiters, quietly tidying shared rooms, or courageously admitting a work mistake — and messaging praising them for it, you bestow upon them a giant invisible gift.

You tell them: *"The kind things you do were by no means forgotten. I saw them."*

This recognition touches straight into recipient's deepest heart part. It instills faith that their kindness and effort hold meaning, granting strength to step forward on their path.

---

## When the Giver Is Also the Receiver

Under Endogenism views, every outward kind action is essentially an internal nourishment process.

Submerged in private anxieties and deadlocks, your mind resembles a closed-door room. You see merely your own troubles, and that leaves your pain inflating abnormally.

The moment actively opening doors, directing attention outward to see and soothe another soul, a miraculous shift occurs.

Your own loneliness begins melting.

Messaging someone kind words, your chest automatically warms up. You sense authentic human-to-human connection — connection transcending calculations and rushing life rhythms.

*Turns out making someone feel warm, my own heart ceases being cold...*

You realize possessing capacity to bring joy to others merely via simple lines. That capacity relies not on money, status, or circumstances. It is a ready internal resource awaiting unleashing.

---

## Let Not Kind Words Lie Still in Thought

How many times in life did you feel love for someone, appreciate someone's effort, yet held that thought still in head and never spoke it?

We are typically shy. We fear appearing cheesy, fear others thinking bad motives, or fear awkward sensations expressing feelings.

And thus kind words float sequentially into oblivion, leaving cold distances between humans living right beside each other.

Courageously overcome that fragile shyness.

This world holds overly much criticism, judgment, and harsh words. Kindness is never redundant, and a sincere praise will never harm anyone.

If right now an image of someone pops into your head — a person having helped you, a person undergoing hardship, or a person you secretly admire — treat it as a heart signal.

Hesitate not. Wait for no perfect future opportunity.

---

## Beginning Right Now with Two Minutes from the Heart

Pick up your phone right now.

Open message apps and search that person's name.

Draft no long paper or polish every artistic word. Write what is truest, most natural flowing in your heart.

Merely one or two short sentences.

*"Hello friend, today I unexpectedly thought of you and wanted to message that I truly treasure our friendship."*

Or: *"I see you working very hard lately. Remember to take care of health, you are doing great already."*

Press send.

And then, place your phone down. Sit not waiting for them to reply immediately or thank loudly. Your action completed fully right in the second the message line dispatched.

You just sowed a warmth seed into this world. And whatever the answer, the world grew a bit gentler, merely thanks to your two kind minutes.
