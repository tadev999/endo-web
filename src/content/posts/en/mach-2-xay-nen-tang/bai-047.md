# Standing Outside the Wheel — When You Observe Your Life Instead of Being Swept into It

*Article 47 / Stream 2: Building Foundations*

---

Have you ever felt your life resembles a giant whirlpool where you are the leaf tightly swept at the center?

Each day begins with rushing alarm sounds, followed by an overflowing task list, urgent response messages, complex relationships, and fluctuating emotional waves. You run from one task to another, reacting to one situation after another hassle, until night falls, you collapse onto bed in a dizzy, head-spinning, and completely exhausted state.

In that state, you are not truly *driving* your life. You are merely *reacting* to it.

You resemble someone sitting on a wooden merry-go-round horse spinning at wind-slashing speed. Lights flash continuously, music screams in ears, and all surrounding scenery blurs into chaotic light streaks. You cannot see anything clearly, cannot think thoroughly, and a nauseous feeling of losing control perpetually threatens explosion.

Stream 2 of the Endogenism journey tells you not to smash the merry-go-round or force the world to stop moving.

Stream 2 invites you to perform an exceptionally vital internal posture shift: **step down off the wooden horse, walk out onto the grass, and stand observing that merry-go-round from a safe distance.**

---

## Metaphor of the Wooden Merry-Go-Round Horse

Re-imagine that brilliant merry-go-round image.

When sitting on the wooden horse's back and spinning wildly to its rhythm, each machine movement becomes your sole reality. When the horse lowers, your heart drops along. When the horse lifts, you feel weightless. You hold no choice other than getting thrown back and forth via the wheel's inertia.

Yet what will occur if you step down off the spinning floor?

The merry-go-round continues operating. Wooden horses keep lifting and lowering. Lights keep flashing and music keeps echoing. Yet right now, you stand under an oak tree's shade outside the hallway.

You are no longer dizzy. You see the machine's full mechanism. You see others screaming on horse backs, see operating gears, and see quiet gaps between rotations.

Most importantly: you realize the merry-go-round is a phenomenon occurring *before your eyes*, rather than *being you*.

Your daily life is precisely such a wheel. Work pressures, colleague remarks, rising anger or sadness — all are wheel movements.

The problem lies not in the wheel itself, but in your having forgotten that you possess full capacity to step outside and stand observing it.

---

## Actor on Stage and Viewer on Balcony

Most of us live our full lives in the role of an **absolutely immersed actor on stage**.

When the script demands anger, you turn into anger. When the script demands suffering, you completely break in pain. You identify yourself one hundred percent with every line, emotion, and crisis in the play. You forget that behind that stage exists a backstage, and up above exists an audience balcony.

The stance of "standing outside the wheel" is precisely your activating the **observer on the balcony** inside yourself.

This observer participates not in arguing with other actors on stage. They merely sit quietly in an elevated position, with a warm tea cup in hand, observing the full play transpiring below.

When a wave of anger erupts inside you over someone's insulting phrase, the actor on stage immediately shouts or reacts cruelly.

Yet the observer on the balcony softly whispers: *Oh, look there. A very strong anger reaction is rising in this person's chest. Heart beating fast, shoulders stiffening. Fascinating observing the body react to those words.*

The difference between these two postures is immense.

The actor says: *"I am anger."*

The observer says: *"I am the vast awareness space where anger is passing through."*

When turning yourself into the containing space rather than emotion itself, anger loses power to manipulate and dictate your actions.

---

## The Life-or-Death Pause Between Stimulus and Response

Great psychologist Viktor Frankl wrote a phrase that became a guidepost for human freedom:

*Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response. In our response lies our growth and our freedom.*

When tightly swept inside the wheel, this space equals zero.

A stimulus appears (a critical email, a mean look, bad news) and your reaction erupts instantly like a knee-jerk reflex mechanism. You respond in panic, anger, or withdrawal. You are a puppet pulled by external circumstances.

When standing outside the wheel, you widen that space.

A stimulus appears. Yet instead of reacting immediately, you step back a pace in your mind. You observe that stimulus. You feel regret or agitation churning in your body. You take a deep breath to the bottom of your abdomen.

And in that brief yet precious few seconds pause, you reclaim cockpit control.

You ask yourself: *Which action right now reflects my true values and foundations? Do I want to respond from cowardly retaliation, or from composure and self-respect?*

The right to choose response is precisely the boundary separating a person living passively by instinct and a person living actively from internal foundations.

---

## Standing Outside Differs from Indifference or Giving Up

At this point, a very natural query might surface in your mind: *If I stand outside observing my life like that, will I become numb, indifferent, and hold no care for life anymore?*

This is a very common misconception regarding the observer attitude.

Standing outside the wheel is no coldness of an ungrateful person or neglect of a defeated resignation. On the contrary, it is the prerequisite for loving and acting most effectively.

Think of a surgeon in an emergency room.

If that surgeon got completely swept into the patient's emotional wheel — if he also panicked, cried, and trembled along with the patient's family — his hands would shake and he couldn't perform a precise incision.

To save the patient's life, the surgeon is compelled to "stand outside that emotional wheel". He maintains supreme quietness and objective observation. That composure originates not from cruelty or apathy, but from deep responsibility toward life.

Similarly, when standing outside your life's wheel, you are no longer blinded by sudden outburst emotions. You see problems as they genuinely are, rather than through fear's magnifying lens.

Then, you can make the wisest career decisions, bestow full listening to children, and offer pure love unaccompanied by manipulation or control.

---

## Daily Practice of Stepping Off the Wheel

How do we practice standing outside the wheel right midst daily busy rush?

You need not pull yourself up high mountains or meditate many hours to accomplish this. You can practice it right at your desk, in gridlocks, or midst a tense meeting.

Apply the **third-person self-addressing technique in mind**.

When noticing yourself falling into supreme stress states, try naming yourself and describing the situation like an outside observer:

*What is happening to Nam right now? Nam is feeling intense pressure over the work deadline. Nam's body is tired and Nam's mind is spinning with failure scenarios.*

Changing address terms in internal dialogue immediately generates a safe psychological distance between you and the issue. You are no longer incarnated into that panic.

Apply the **three-breath pause rule**.

Before clicking send on a angry response email, before uttering argument words with loved ones, or before making a major decision in a rush — force yourself to pause for exactly three breaths.

Inhale deeply, feel air flow through nose down to abdomen, and exhale slowly. Those three breaths serve as the bridge bringing you down off the spinning wooden horse back to solid ground beneath your feet.

---

## Becoming the Quiet Space

Life's weather will always alter.

There will be brilliant sunny days of success and praise. There will be dark stormy days of failure, rejection, and unavoidable losses.

If viewing yourself as weather, you will shatter with every storm.

Yet if realizing you are the **vast sky containing that weather**, you will maintain unshakeable peace. Dark clouds may pull in covering the sky, blizzards may rage, yet the sky space itself was never harmed or shattered by blizzards.

Permit events, emotions, and thoughts to transpire on your life's stage. Watch them, experience them, yet never forget your true location: the quiet observer on the balcony.

When mastering the stance of standing outside the wheel, you will discover a true internal freedom.

The surrounding world can keep spinning and noisy as it is, yet right at your chest center, a quiet and self-composed candle flame keeps burning serenely, where no wind can ever extinguish it.
