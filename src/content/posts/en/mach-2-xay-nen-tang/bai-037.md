# Clearing the Mind Is Not Forcing Thoughts Away — It Is Recognizing Which One You Are Holding On To

*Article 37 / Stream 2: Building Foundations*

---

Have you ever climbed into bed after a long exhausting day, longing for deep sleep to restore energy, but the moment your head hits the pillow, a sound festival erupts in your head?

Hundreds of messy thought streams queue up to appear without your permission.

A foolish phrase you blurted out 3 years ago, a hypothetical argument with a colleague tomorrow, vague anxiety over money, comparing yourself with someone on social media, or a series of tragic scenarios wove by your own mind.

You feel your head like a cramped, cluttered room compressed so tight it chokes your breath.

In that helplessness, your natural reflex is striving to force yourself.

*Stop thinking! Shut off the head! Be quiet!*

Yet an ironic reality occurs: The more you try to force thoughts away, the denser, more rushing, and more aggressive they appear.

Trying to clear the mind by destroying thoughts resembles trying to use a stick to smash water waves — the more you smash, the more violently the water surface ruffles.

Why is that? Endogenous philosophy and Mindfulness psychology offer a liberating discovery.

An overburdened and cramped mind is not because too many thoughts appear, but because your Ego is continuously grabbing, clinging to, and holding on to those thoughts.

Clearing the mind is not a war enforcing silence. Clearing the mind is the art of recognizing what you are holding on to, and learning to open the mind's palm to return all thoughts to their natural flowing order.

---

## The White Bear Effect and Brain Mechanisms

To untangle clutter in the head, we must first comprehend the brain's operating mechanism when falling into the trap of enforcing quietness.

In experimental psychology, a very famous study exists called the White Bear Effect.

When researchers requested people to sit still and instructed *Think about anything you want, but absolutely do not think about a white bear*, the outcome was exceedingly ironic.

Precisely the instruction not to think caused the white bear image to appear in their heads with far denser frequency.

The reason is the human brain operates under a subconscious monitoring system: when you command not to think about something, the brain immediately runs a process to continuously scan whether that thing is appearing. And precisely that continuous scanning behavior triggers the image to appear.

Thinking is a natural physiological function of the brain.

Just as the heart is designed to beat, lungs designed to breathe, stomach designed to contract — your brain is designed to produce thoughts.

A healthy brain will continuously generate thousands of thoughts, memories, and associations each day. That is no system error, still less a sign that you are incompetent or lacking discipline.

Your anger or panic when the brain produces thoughts is as illogical as your anger over why your heart beats.

The error lies not in thoughts appearing; the error lies in how we react and cling to those thoughts.

## From Natural Thoughts to Suffering Clinging

Let us distinguish two completely different states taking place in your mind.

The first state is natural thought arising. That is a thought stream flashing up and drifting past your mind like a ripple or a cloud in the sky. This is completely natural and objective.

The second state is the origin of suffering: thought clinging. That is the moment your Ego reaches out its hand to grab that ripple, assigns it a personal label, identifies self with it, and begins weaving a long drama story.

Look at a daily example.

A thought flashes in your head: *This afternoon the boss said that phrase, looks like the boss is dissatisfied with me.*

If you let it drift past like a cloud, this thought will automatically vanish after a few seconds.

But when clinging surfaces, you grab that thought and begin identifying with it: *Oh my god, boss dissatisfied means I'm about to get laid off? If laid off where's money for rent? I'm such a useless person! Why do I always ruin everything?*

Do you see the difference?

Your mind is not cluttered by the boss's phrase itself. It is accumulated and overburdened by tons of junk furniture your Ego wove and stored immediately after that initial thought.

Mind clutter is precisely the result of our collecting all passing thoughts and storing them inside our internal house, instead of opening doors for them to pass freely.

## Metaphors of the Autumn River and the Hoarder's House

To visualize the mind clearing process gently, let us contemplate two metaphors below.

Imagine your mind as a broad, calm river flowing through an autumn forest. Your thoughts, emotions, memories, and anxieties are yellow leaves falling and floating on the water surface.

Two ways exist for you to treat this river.

The way of resistance and clinging is jumping down standing in the riverbed. Whenever seeing a black leaf representing negative thoughts floating near, you jump up frantically, reach out trying to catch the leaf, crush it or clutch it to your chest crying out: *Why is this leaf so black? Why did it float into my river?*

The outcome is you muddy the entire water stream, and your hands clutch rotting leaves.

The way of mindfulness and endogenous clearing is stepping up to stand on the riverbank. You become a relaxed observer.

You see a yellow leaf float past — you know it is a yellow leaf. You see a black leaf float past — you know it is a black leaf. You do not reach down to fish out any leaf. You let the water current naturally carry each leaf far away.

The river is never clogged by passing leaves. It is only clogged when you build dams to hold those leaves back.

Clearing the mind is simply stepping onto the riverbank and ceasing to fish out leaves.

Now, imagine your mind as a house. When you were born, that house was spacious, airy, bursting with light and fresh air.

Yet through years, whenever an anxious thought, someone's criticism, fear of failure, or resentment appeared, you picked it up, boxed it in a cardboard box, and stacked it in a room corner.

You kept everything: from parents' expectations, friend comparisons, to hypothetical conversations that never occurred.

After many years, your mind house is so packed with trash cardboard boxes that you lack space to breathe, lack room to sit down and relax.

Clearing that house is not using a gun to blast cardboard boxes or forcing thoughts away.

Clearing the house is opening all windows, lifting each cardboard box, looking at it with compassion, and gently carrying it outside.

You realize this thing no longer belongs to you, and you need not keep it.

## Three Tools for Opening the Mind's Palm

So how do we cease clinging and clear the mind naturally in daily life?

The first tool is the thought labeling technique.

When seeing your mind begin chaotically spinning, instead of getting drawn into the story content, step back a pace and call out the nature of that thought type.

When recognizing yourself worrying about the future, whisper in your head: *Oh, this is an anxious thought. My mind is turning on tragedy prediction mode again.*

When recognizing yourself agonizing over the past, identify: *This is a regretful thought. This is an old record playing back.*

When recognizing yourself comparing with others, observe: *This is an Ego comparison thought.*

The act of labeling functions as a psychological brake. It immediately shifts you from inside the thought to being the thought observer. When you recognize this is merely a thought, you strip away most of its manipulative power over your emotions.

The second tool is shifting from identification to cognitive defusion.

Look at the difference between the two sentences below in your mind.

When identified, you tell yourself *I am a pathetic failure.* You and the thought merge as one, you treat the thought as absolute truth.

When defused cognitively, you gently adjust *I notice I am having the thought that I am a failure.*

When adding the phrase *I notice I am having the thought that...* before any negative thought, you create a safe cognitive gap.

A thought is merely a temporary psychological event in the brain, it is not your authentic essence or intrinsic value. You are not your thoughts; you are the quiet space observing those thoughts pass.

The third tool is practicing opening the palm.

Whenever finding yourself clinging stubbornly to a worry, anger, or expectation: sit down, join both hands and clench them into tight fists before your chest.

Feel the tension, pain, and exhaustion of hand muscles having to clench continuously. That is precisely what your mind undergoes when clinging to thoughts.

Then, slowly take a deep breath, and while exhaling, slowly open both hands, turning palms facing upward.

Relax all fingers. Say to your mind: *I do not need to clench this anymore. I open my palm to release control. Let everything freely come and freely go.*

The body's relaxation response will immediately spread to the mind, bringing a miraculous ease and peace.

## The Quietness Always There

A massive misunderstanding exists that quietness is something we must struggle to create or seek in a far temple or remote mountain peak.

Endogenous philosophy reminds you that the quiet sky was always there behind layers of clouds.

Messy thoughts, minor anxieties are merely storm clouds temporarily obscuring the sky. The sky needs not fight clouds, needs not force clouds away.

The sky merely needs to continue being the sky — vast, quiet, open — and let storms come and dissipate on their own.

When you cease forcing thoughts away, when you learn to identify what the Ego strives to hold and gently open your hand, you will realize a liberating truth.

Your mind was never truly shattered or cramped. Your quietness, clarity, and intrinsic value remain intact right there, waiting for you to return and dwell in each present moment.

Today, if your head occasionally becomes noisy — do not be angry with yourself. Smile, step onto the riverbank, open your palm, and whisper:

*I see this thought. And I choose to let it freely float away.*
